Mood Tracker Support

Last updated: 1 November 2026

Mood Tracker is a private journal for checking in with yourself. You decide what it asks you, and everything you write stays on your phone.

If you cannot find an answer below, email apps@kalob.io.

Getting started

The first time you open the app it asks what you want to track and offers three starting points:

  • The full check-in — mood, fatigue, loneliness, movement, thoughts of self-harm, and three food habits. Fifteen questions.
  • Just mood — a 0–10 slider, a word for how you feel, and a note. Three questions.
  • Start from scratch — an empty form you build yourself.

Whichever you pick, every question can be changed, reordered or removed afterwards. Nothing is locked in.

Every question is optional to answer. Fill in one thing or all of them; the entry saves either way.

Changing your questions

Tap the gear icon on the Log tab, then Your form.

  • Add a question — tap Add a field and choose a type: a slider, a word choice, a description, a date and time, or a yes/no.
  • Edit one — tap it. There is a live preview at the bottom showing exactly how it will look.
  • Reorder — use the up and down arrows on the right of each row.
  • Group questions together — give two or more the same Section name and they share a single card, the way Mood holds both a slider and a word.

Renaming a question

Renaming updates it everywhere, including entries you saved months ago. Your answers are stored against the question itself, not its name, so nothing is lost or orphaned.

Removing a question

Tap the question, then Remove this field. You get two choices:

  • Hide from new check-ins — it stops appearing on the Log tab, but your old answers stay readable in History. This is the safe option.
  • Erase answers too — permanently deletes every answer you ever gave it. The app tells you how many entries that affects before you confirm, and asks again.

Hidden questions are listed under Removed fields in Your form, and you can restore one at any time.

Trends

Only slider questions can be charted, and only if Show on the Trends chart is switched on in that question's settings. If a slider is missing from the chart, that is almost always why.

Tap a name in the legend to hide or show its line. Touch and drag across the chart to read a single day.

When you create a slider you choose which end is good news. Fatigue, for example, is better when it is low, so the app colours it accordingly and counts a fall as an improvement.

Reminders

Three reminders a day, at 9:00 AM, 2:00 PM and 8:00 PM by default. Change any of them under Settings › Reminder times, or switch them off entirely.

My reminders stopped arriving

This is almost always the expected behaviour rather than a fault. iOS only lets an app queue a limited number of scheduled notifications, so the app books about twenty days of reminders in advance and tops the queue up every time you open it.

If you do not open the app for roughly three weeks, the queue runs out and reminders stop until you open it again. Opening the app once refills them immediately.

If they stopped sooner than that, check:

  • Notifications are allowed in iOS Settings › Mood Tracker › Notifications.
  • Focus or Do Not Disturb is not filtering them.
  • The toggle in Settings › Reminder times is on.

That screen also has Send a test reminder, which fires one three seconds later so you can confirm the whole chain works. Background the app after tapping it.

Backing up, and moving to a new phone

Because nothing is stored on a server, there is no account to sign into on a new phone. Your journal moves in one of two ways.

Automatically, with your device backup

Your entries are part of your normal iPhone backup. Restoring a new phone from an iCloud or computer backup brings the app's data with it.

Manually, with an export file

Go to Settings › Privacy & data › Export a backup. The app writes a readable file and hands it to the share sheet, so you choose where it goes — Files, email to yourself, or a cloud service.

To bring it back, use Import a backup on the new phone and pick the file. You are offered two options:

  • Replace everything — discards whatever is on this phone and restores the file exactly. Use this on a fresh install.
  • Add missing entries — keeps what is here and only adds entries this phone does not already have. Use this to merge two devices.

Exporting regularly is worth the ten seconds. If you lose the phone and have no backup, the journal is genuinely gone — that is the trade-off for nothing being stored anywhere else.

Deleting things

  • One entry — open it from History and tap Delete entry.
  • Everything — Settings › Privacy & data › Erase everything. This removes your form and every entry, and returns you to the first-run screen.
  • The whole app — deleting it from your home screen removes the database with it.

None of these can be undone unless you exported a backup first.

Privacy

There is no account, no sign-in, and no server holding your entries. The full detail, including the one network request the app does make and what it contains, is in the privacy policy.

Reporting a problem

Email apps@kalob.io. It helps to include:

  • What you were doing when it happened, and what you expected instead.
  • Your iOS version and iPhone model.
  • The app version, shown at the bottom of the App Store listing.

Please do not send your journal or a backup file. It is not needed to diagnose a problem, and I would rather not have a copy of it.

A note on the self-harm question

The starter form includes a space for thoughts of self-harm, because noticing a pattern can be useful. It is optional like everything else, and you can remove it under Your form if you would rather not be asked.

Mood Tracker is a personal journal, not a medical service, and nobody reads what you write. If things feel urgent, please contact your local emergency services. In the United States and Canada you can call or text 988 to reach a crisis counsellor.
